## Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants convert sunlight, water, and
carbon dioxide into glucose and oxygen. It takes place primarily in the
chloroplasts of plant cells, where the pigment chlorophyll absorbs light energy.
The light-dependent reactions occur in the thylakoid membranes, while the
Calvin cycle takes place in the stroma.

## Mitochondria

Mitochondria are membrane-bound organelles found in most eukaryotic cells. They
are often called the powerhouse of the cell because they generate most of the
cell's supply of adenosine triphosphate (ATP) through oxidative phosphorylation.
Mitochondria contain their own circular DNA, separate from the cell's nuclear
DNA, which suggests an endosymbiotic origin.

## The Roman Empire

The Roman Empire was founded in 27 BC when Augustus became the first emperor
after the fall of the Roman Republic. At its height under Emperor Trajan around
117 AD, it stretched from Britain in the north to Egypt in the south. The
Western Roman Empire fell in 476 AD when the Germanic king Odoacer deposed the
last emperor Romulus Augustulus.

## The French Revolution

The French Revolution began in 1789 with the storming of the Bastille on July
14. It abolished the monarchy, executed King Louis XVI in 1793, and gave rise
to the Reign of Terror under Maximilien Robespierre. The revolution introduced
the principles of liberty, equality, and fraternity that shaped modern
democracy.

## Quantum Mechanics

Quantum mechanics is the branch of physics that describes nature at the
smallest scales of energy levels of atoms and subatomic particles. It was
developed in the early twentieth century by physicists including Max Planck,
Albert Einstein, Niels Bohr, Werner Heisenberg, and Erwin Schrödinger. Its core
ideas include wave-particle duality, the uncertainty principle, and quantum
superposition.

## The Internet

The internet is a global network of interconnected computer networks that uses
the TCP/IP protocol suite. It originated from ARPANET, which was developed by
the United States Department of Defense in the late 1960s. The World Wide Web,
invented by Tim Berners-Lee in 1989, is built on top of the internet and
introduced the use of hyperlinks and web browsers.

## Black Holes

A black hole is a region of spacetime where gravity is so strong that nothing,
not even light, can escape from it. The boundary of a black hole is called the
event horizon. The first image of a black hole, located at the centre of the
galaxy M87, was published in April 2019 by the Event Horizon Telescope
collaboration.

## DNA

Deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) is the molecule that carries genetic information in
all known living organisms. It has a double-helix structure, discovered by
James Watson and Francis Crick in 1953, with help from Rosalind Franklin's
X-ray diffraction images. DNA consists of four nucleotide bases: adenine,
thymine, cytosine, and guanine.

## Climate Change

Climate change refers to long-term shifts in temperatures and weather patterns,
primarily caused by human activities since the Industrial Revolution. The
burning of fossil fuels releases greenhouse gases, especially carbon dioxide
and methane, which trap heat in the Earth's atmosphere. The Paris Agreement,
signed in 2015, aims to limit global warming to well below 2 degrees Celsius
above pre-industrial levels.

## The Renaissance

The Renaissance was a cultural and intellectual movement that began in Italy
in the 14th century and spread across Europe over the following three
centuries. It marked a renewed interest in classical Greek and Roman art,
literature, and philosophy. Key figures of the Renaissance include Leonardo da
Vinci, Michelangelo, and Raphael.

## Albert Einstein

Albert Einstein was a German-born theoretical physicist who developed the
theory of relativity, one of the two pillars of modern physics. He published
the special theory of relativity in 1905 and the general theory of relativity
in 1915. Einstein received the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1921 for his
discovery of the photoelectric effect.

## The Pacific Ocean

The Pacific Ocean is the largest and deepest ocean on Earth. It covers about
46 percent of the Earth's water surface and roughly one-third of its total
surface area. The deepest point in the Pacific, and on Earth, is the
Challenger Deep in the Mariana Trench, which is nearly 11 kilometres deep.

## Vaccines

Vaccines are biological preparations that provide active acquired immunity to
a particular infectious disease. They typically contain an agent that
resembles a disease-causing microorganism and stimulate the body's immune
system to recognise it as a threat. Edward Jenner pioneered the smallpox
vaccine in 1796, which led to the eventual eradication of smallpox in 1980.

## The Great Wall of China

The Great Wall of China is a series of fortifications built across the
historical northern borders of ancient Chinese states and Imperial China. The
most well-known sections were built by the Ming dynasty between 1368 and 1644.
The wall stretches over 13 000 miles in total length when all branches and
sections are counted.

## Shakespeare

William Shakespeare was an English playwright and poet, widely regarded as the
greatest writer in the English language. He wrote 39 plays and 154 sonnets
between roughly 1589 and 1613. His best-known works include Hamlet, Macbeth,
King Lear, and Romeo and Juliet.

## Volcanoes

A volcano is a rupture in the crust of a planetary-mass object that allows
hot lava, volcanic ash, and gases to escape from a magma chamber below the
surface. The Pacific Ring of Fire contains about 75 percent of the world's
active volcanoes. Mount Vesuvius, which famously destroyed the Roman city of
Pompeii in 79 AD, is one of the most dangerous volcanoes in the world.

## Artificial Intelligence

Artificial intelligence is intelligence demonstrated by machines, in contrast
to the natural intelligence of humans and animals. The field was founded as
an academic discipline in 1956 at the Dartmouth Workshop. Major subfields
include machine learning, natural language processing, computer vision, and
robotics.

## The Solar System

The solar system consists of the Sun and the objects gravitationally bound to
it, including eight planets and their moons, dwarf planets, asteroids, and
comets. The four inner planets — Mercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars — are
terrestrial. The four outer planets — Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune —
are giant planets.

## The Industrial Revolution

The Industrial Revolution was the transition to new manufacturing processes in
Europe and the United States, occurring roughly between 1760 and 1840. It
introduced steam power, mechanised textile production, and the factory
system. The revolution began in Britain and dramatically increased
productivity and living standards over time.

## The Olympic Games

The modern Olympic Games are an international sporting event held every four
years, alternating between Summer and Winter Games. They were revived by
Pierre de Coubertin in 1896 in Athens, inspired by the ancient Greek Olympic
Games held at Olympia from 776 BC. The Olympic motto is "Citius, Altius,
Fortius" — Latin for "Faster, Higher, Stronger".
